Data protection & safeguarding

Safety and privacy aren't add-ons. They are the product.

To match families with sitters, Aire handles some of the most sensitive information a platform can hold: where children live, who looks after them and when, background-check records and payment details. A single breach or misuse could put a child at risk, so every design choice is tested against our promise of safety, convenience and trust.

Privacy by design

Safety settings are on by default. Users never have to hunt for them.

Minimum data, minimum time

If we don't need it to run a booking, we don't collect it. Every data type is deleted automatically once its retention period ends.

Need to know

People only see the data needed for a booking they are part of.

Reveal late

A family's full home address is released only once a booking is confirmed, shortly before it starts.

Never sold

We never sell data. Children's data is never used for profiling or advertising.

Plain language

Privacy notices are short, written for parents and sitters, and available in the app.

Verification & vetting workflow

Garda vetting is carried out only for registered organisations through the National Vetting Bureau. Aire will either register as a relevant organisation (Option A) or partner with an already-registered vetting provider (Option B).

Account

The sitter creates an account and verifies their email and phone.

Identity

The sitter uploads photo ID, which is matched to a live selfie.

Garda vetting

The sitter completes the vetting application through our approved route.

Certificates

Irish Red Cross first aid and Children First certificates are checked by an admin to make sure they're valid and in date.

Go live

The profile becomes searchable only once every check passes. Badges are removed automatically when anything expires.

What parents see

Badges show status only, never the underlying documents. We store the verification outcome and date rather than the full disclosure wherever the law allows.

Minimum age

Sitters must be 16 or older. Sitters aged 16–17 need a parent or guardian's consent for vetting and use of the app, which reflects Ireland's digital age of consent of 16. Sitters aged 18 and over can take bookings on their own.

Parents are verified too

Parents verify their identity and phone number, so sitters know who they are visiting.

Safeguards by user group

Children

  • No child accounts. Children appear only as a nickname and age bracket in a booking.
  • No photos, surnames, schools or birthdays are stored.
  • Care notes are seen only by the confirmed sitter and deleted 30 days after the booking.
  • No child information is ever passed to Uber.
  • A one-tap emergency button, a report button and a named safeguarding lead.
  • A sitter can be suspended immediately on a credible report.

Parents

  • Profiles show only the approximate area.
  • Messages stay in the app, so no numbers are exchanged before booking.
  • Tokenised Stripe payments. Aire never sees full card numbers.
  • Routines and booking times are visible only to the booked sitter.
  • Optional two-factor authentication and alerts for new devices.
  • View, download and delete your data in the app.

Sitters

  • ID and vetting documents are kept in a separate locked store.
  • A sitter's home address and phone number are never shown to parents.
  • Sitters see the family's verified status first and can decline with no penalty.
  • Uber link, trusted-contact sharing, and report and block buttons.
  • Reviews are moderated, and abusive reviews are removed.
  • Fees are shown clearly before a booking is accepted.

Technical security measures

Encryption

TLS in transit. Strong encryption at rest, with extra encryption for ID and vetting documents.

EU hosting

Hosted in an EU-based cloud region under a signed data processing agreement.

Access control

Role-based access with MFA. Every admin access to personal data is logged and reviewed.

Pseudonymisation

Internal analytics use user IDs, never names or addresses.

Secure development

Code review, dependency scanning and no secrets in code.

Testing

A penetration test before launch and at least once a year after that.

Backups

Encrypted, tested backups with a documented restore process.

Anti-bypass

Message filters flag phone numbers and requests to pay off the app.

Legal & regulatory framework

GDPR & Data Protection Act 2018

A lawful basis for every use of data, transparency, the rights of access, correction and deletion, and breach notification to the Data Protection Commission within 72 hours.

Children's data

Treated as high risk. We follow the DPC's child-focused guidance and complete a DPIA before launch.

Special category & criminal data

Health care notes and vetting outcomes are kept to a minimum, with restricted access and a clear legal basis for each.

National Vetting Bureau Acts 2012–2016

The biggest compliance question for the app is which vetting route to use: register directly or partner with a registered organisation.

Children First Act 2015

Child-safeguarding practices and a clear route to report concerns to Tusla and An Garda Síochána.

Payments & digital consent

Card data is handled by PCI DSS certified Stripe. Ireland's digital age of consent is 16.

If personal data is lost or exposed

Contain

Revoke access and isolate the affected systems.

Assess

Work out which data and users are affected. Children's data and addresses come first.

Notify

Tell the Data Protection Commission within 72 hours where there is a risk to people.

Inform & fix

Tell affected users plainly, record what happened and fix the cause.

Safeguarding incidents

Child safe first

Contact emergency services if needed.

Suspend

Suspend the accounts involved while the report is reviewed.

Report

Report to Tusla and An Garda Síochána through the safeguarding lead.

Preserve & review

Keep records secure for investigators and remove accounts permanently if needed.

Note: this is a project planning summary written by students and is not legal advice. Legal references should be confirmed with a qualified solicitor and the Data Protection Commission before launch.
